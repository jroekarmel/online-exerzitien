Father Jacques of Jesus

Father Jacques of Jesus (1900–1945), a Carmelite and French priest, hid Jewish children during the Second World War, was arrested, and died shortly after the liberation of a concentration camp.

Brief Biography

Little Lucien, born on 29 January 1900 into a poor family whose daily life was marked by hard work, very soon dreamed of becoming “a great parish priest.” The dechristianized regions and children left to fend for themselves awakened and strengthened his passion as an apostle and educator.

A few months before his ordination to the diaconate, he was appointed to Collège Saint-Joseph in Le Havre, where he discovered the Carmel. On 11 July 1925, he was ordained a priest.

After many inner and outward struggles, he left the Diocese of Rouen with the consent of his archbishop and entered the novitiate of the Discalced Carmelites in Lille in September 1932. He received the name Jacques of Jesus.

He founded and directed the small Collège Sainte-Thérèse-de-l’Enfant-Jésus in Avon, where he developed the many educational gifts of his vocation as an educator. He wanted “his” children to grow into complete human beings, aware of their future responsibilities and carrying within themselves the seed of holiness …

His heart as a man and priest was wounded by the cruel acts of the Nazi regime. Deeply shaken, he took the side of those who were suffering and persecuted.

In January 1943, he took in three Jewish children. On 15 January 1944, the Gestapo arrested the three children and Father Jacques. Before he left, he said these words: “Goodbye, children. Carry on without me!”
